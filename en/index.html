04 — trip basics

How to get here, when to go
and what it costs

Blagoveshchensk is 5,595 km east of Moscow and six time zones away. Here's how people get here, which season fits which mood, and the sums to plan around.

Getting there

Three roads into the city on the Amur
By plane7.5–8 h
Direct flights from Moscow — 5,595 km, from 7 h 25 min. Ignatyevo airport (BQS) is 15 km from the centre: city bus No. 8, suburban buses 503 and 510 to the coach station, a taxi 20–30 minutes. Moscow is 6 hours behind, so add a day to adjust after the flight.
By train~6 days
The Trans-Siberian bypasses the city: the nearest hub is Belogorsk, about 110 km away. From there a bus or a local train to Blagoveshchensk, 2–2.5 hours. For those travelling for the journey, not the speed.
From China, across the Amurminutes
The Russia–China road bridge at Kani-Kurgan with passenger buses; a ferry in summer, a pontoon crossing in winter. And the world's first cross-border cable car, Blagoveshchensk–Heihe, due to open in late 2026 — 6–8 minutes over the border.

December – February

Winter

−15 … −27 °C
The Amur freezes and a pontoon crossing to China opens over the ice. A rink on the embankment, Heihe's lights across the river, empty museums. The cold is dry but serious — bring proper gear.

Spring

Ice drift on the Amur
The season's big spectacle is the ice drift, usually in late April (in 2026 the ice between Blagoveshchensk and Heihe broke on 19 April, ahead of schedule). The city wakes up, tourists are still few, prices are calm.

Summer

In September the city hosts a film festival: premieres, theatre, stars on the embankment. Golden taiga all around, comfortable warmth early in the season, and far fewer people than in summer.

05 — two countries in a day

Russia in the morning,
China by noon

Heihe is right there from the embankment — 750 metres of water. No visa needed, just a passport: reaching the neighbouring country is easier and cheaper than the next region over. Let's price it out.

No visa needed — but the trip isn't free

Since 15 September 2025 Russians enter China visa-free for up to 30 days; in May 2026 the arrangement was extended to 31 December 2027. All you need is a passport valid for at least 6 more months — no invitation, hotel booking or paperwork. It's the visa that's free: the crossing over the Amur itself is paid, and that's the main expense.

By riverboat

~6,300 ₽
season · May – October

The classic option: 10–15 minutes on the water plus formalities. Departures from Blagoveshchensk at 10:00, 12:00, 14:00 and 16:00 Amur time. 3,780 ₽ over, 2,520 ₽ back. In the off-season, before the ice sets, hovercraft run — pricier.

By bus over the bridge

~5,300 ₽
year-round

Over the first Russia–China road bridge. First departure from the "Amurasso" coach station at 07:30, in Heihe by 09:50; last one out of Blagoveshchensk at 14:10. About 2,650 ₽ one way, ~2 h 20 min including the border. In winter a pontoon crossing runs too.

By cable car

soon
opening — late 2026

The world's first cross-border cable car over a state frontier. Cabins hold up to 110 people, 6–8 minutes above the Amur. The cable was strung across the river in July 2026; opening is set for the end of the year — fares not yet announced.

A green city

An hour back in time

Heihe runs on Beijing time, Blagoveshchensk on Amur time. Crossing the river you gain an hour; coming back you lose it. Crossing timetables are in Amur time.

Haggle

In markets and malls the price is a starting point, not a final one. Knocking off 10–30% is normal. Without haggling you simply overpay.

The way back is slower

The border is quick on the way in, slower on the way out — they inspect your shopping. Leave a buffer and don't cut it close with the last departure.

Why people go

Markets and electronics, Dongbei food, spas and massage, a promenade looking back at Russia, a wetlands park. And a category of its own — all-in-one dentistry and cosmetic procedures.

06 — coming soon

The world's first cable car
between two countries

A cable car is being strung across the Amur from Russia to China — the only one of its kind anywhere. Instead of a ferry or a bus with formalities, a cabin and a few minutes above the border river. Construction began in 2019 and is now in its final stretch.

What the terminal will hold

A four-storey building on the embankment: border and customs control, duty-free shops, a retail gallery and a restaurant overlooking the Amur. Planned hours are 07:00 to 23:00 daily. The project costs around 10 billion roubles.

Commissioning and readiness for operation
Honestly about the timing: it has slipped before. First the opening was promised for spring 2026, then a test run in July. Per the latest — Heilongjiang TV, 5 July 2026 — commissioning will happen at the end of the year, and the facility will be ready for operation in December. So if you're planning to ride over the Amur, count on winter, not autumn.

The fare hasn't been announced officially. Reports cite a ballpark of about 3,000 ₽ for a round trip, but that's an estimate, not a tariff.

09 — food

The cuisine of two countries on one table

The Dongbei cooking of neighbouring Heihe has long been everyday fare here — alongside Amur river fish, soy from Russia's "soy capital", wild taiga harvests and a Cossack heritage.

10 — curious facts

The Blagoveshchensk you don't expect

Facts, oddities and city lore that turn a trip into a story — from those who've already been to this border between two worlds.

750 metres — and you're in another world
From the embankment you can watch Chinese Heihe go about its day. The world's only pair of cities of two countries standing this close: their own language, food, money and way of life — across a single river.
Nearly every car is right-hand drive
About 90% of cars are right-hand drive, and the taxi driver sits on the right. A Far Eastern habit that visitors get used to within a day.
A monument to the shuttle trader
In the 1990s the "bricks" — human couriers — hauled over 30 tonnes of goods a day across the Amur. Shuttle trading kept the city afloat when the economy stalled. It got its own monument.
The yuan flipped everything
When the yuan cost 3–5 ₽, Russians went to Heihe for everything. Now the yuan is 12–13 ₽ — and it's the Chinese who come to shop in Blagoveshchensk.
Druzhok, the embankment dog
In the 2013 flood the Amur rose to a record 822 cm (over 8 metres) and swamped 126 settlements in the region. A dog waited days for his owners on the porch of a flooded house — now a bronze Druzhok "guards the whole city".
The arch went up in half a year
The Triumphal Arch was raised for the visit of Tsarevich Nicholas. Similar ones went up in Khabarovsk and Vladivostok, but Blagoveshchensk's is the largest.
A cable car from country to country
The world's first cross-border cable car over the Amur. Cabins hold up to 110 people each, and it's just a few minutes over the river between two states.
A balalaika meets a pipa
In Friendship Park, behind a Chinese pagoda stands a Soviet apartment block, and beside it — a monument to the Russian balalaika and the Chinese pipa. Peace, friendship, music.
To China for your health
People fly to Heihe from across Russia for all-in-one dentistry and beauty treatments: cheaper, guaranteed, and the full range of services in one centre.
A portion is at least 400 grams
In Chinese cafés dishes are ordered for everyone and placed in the middle of the table. Nothing comes under 400 g — two people finishing three dishes is nearly impossible.
When cannon stood on the embankment
In 1900, during the Boxer Rebellion in China, the border by the city briefly became a line of armed standoff for a couple of months. Today that same bank has strolls and fountains.
A fountain you walk through
On Lenin Square stands Eurasia's largest walk-through light-and-music fountain: nearly 3,000 m² of jets rise straight from the pavement. By day children dash through it; by night, a water-and-light show. The season opens on 1 May.
Sunshine is the norm here
By climate references Blagoveshchensk has about 186 sunny days a year — more than half the calendar. July delivers up to 23 sunny days, and the frosty winter is almost always clear-skied.
An AI censor in the museum
At the museum they asked China's DeepSeek about that conflict — it politely declined to answer. ChatGPT told all. One question, different countries, different answers.
11 — questions

What people ask before the trip

Quick answers to the things people check first. Details are in the sections above.

Do I need a visa to visit Heihe?
No. Since 15 September 2025 Russians enter China visa-free for up to 30 days; in May 2026 this was extended to the end of 2027. You only need a passport valid for at least 6 more months — no invitations, bookings or paperwork. The crossing itself is paid: about 6,300 ₽ round trip by riverboat, about 5,300 ₽ by bus over the bridge.
How do I get to Blagoveshchensk from Moscow?
By direct flight — 5,595 km, from 7 h 25 min. Tickets from ~10,000 ₽ one way if booked early; young people, pensioners, large families and Far East residents get a subsidised fare of 9,700 ₽. By train — to Belogorsk on the Trans-Siberian (~110 km), then a bus or local train, 2–2.5 hours. Moscow is 6 hours behind.
When is the best time to visit Blagoveshchensk?
The safe answer is summer: the embankment, riverboats, lotuses, +22…+28 °C. But each season has its trump card: late April brings the ice drift on the Amur, September the "Amur Autumn" film festival and mellow warmth, winter a frozen river, a skating rink and Heihe's lights across the ice at −15…−27 °C.
When do the lotuses bloom, and where to see them?
From mid-July to mid-August, peaking in early August. The closest spot to the city is a lake near the village of Ivanovka, about an hour by car. The Komarov lotus is protected: no picking, but photograph all you like.
Is it true that nearly all cars are right-hand drive?
True: about 90% of the city's cars are right-hand drive — Japanese vehicles are a Far Eastern staple. Your taxi driver will sit on the right. Visitors adjust in a day; for locals it's just normal.
How much does a day in Heihe cost?
Ballpark per person: the crossing 5,300–6,300 ₽ round trip, lunch at a Chinese café 400–800 ₽, shopping to taste (haggling is a must — 10–30% off). A "see and eat" day comes to roughly 8,000–10,000 ₽. A night's stay adds from 1,300 ₽ per room.
When will the cable car to China open?
As of July 2026 — December 2026: the cables are strung across the Amur, it's about 90% complete, with commissioning at year's end. The date has slipped before, so double-check before your trip. For now the riverboat and the bridge bus are running.
How do I pay in Heihe?
Russian Visa and Mastercard don't work in China. Bring cash yuan — you can exchange it at banks in Blagoveshchensk. In Heihe's Russia-facing tourist spots roubles are often accepted too, but the rate is worse. The Chinese themselves mostly pay by Alipay and WeChat Pay.

01 · start

The Triumphal Arch

The grand approach to the Amur starts here. The twenty-metre arch with double-headed eagles was raised in 1891 for Tsarevich Nicholas's visit and recreated in 2005. Beyond it open the river and the Chinese bank.

02

The Muravyov-Amursky monument

The city's founder and "gatherer" of the Amur region stands on the embankment, looking out at the Amur with China beyond. It was he who signed the 1858 Treaty of Aigun that returned these lands to Russia.

History
03

Facing Heihe — 750 metres

Here the Amur narrows to 750 metres, and from the parapet you can see Chinese Heihe: towers, a promenade, evening lights. The world's only pair of cities of two countries standing this close — eye to eye across the river.

04

Druzhok the dog

A bronze dog on the parapet — a memory of the 2013 flood, when the Amur rose above 8 metres. He waited days for his owners on the porch of a flooded house; now, locals say, he "guards the whole city".

05 · finale

Lenin Square and the singing fountain

The walk leads to the city's heart: Eurasia's largest walk-through light-and-music fountain rises straight from the pavement — nearly 3,000 m² of jets. By day children dash through it; by night, a water-and-light show set to music.
